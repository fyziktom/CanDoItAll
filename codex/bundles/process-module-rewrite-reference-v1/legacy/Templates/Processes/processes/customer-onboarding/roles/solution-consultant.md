# Solution consultant

**Key:** `solution-consultant`  
**Scope:** local  
**Process:** customer-onboarding  
**Preferred executor:** person  
**Preferred project role:** TechnicalContact  
**Seniority:** Senior solution consulting  
**Minimum years in primary discipline:** 5  
**Minimum years in software delivery:** 7

## Summary
Configuration and fit-gap specialist for mapping customer needs to the delivered product capability.

## Purpose
Translate customer operating reality into a safe, supportable configuration and adoption path.

## Staffing intent
A solution specialist who understands both the product’s capabilities and the customer’s working model.

## Snapshot summary
Configuration and fit-gap specialist for mapping customer needs to the delivered product capability.

## Domain tags
configuration, fit-gap, customer-solutioning

## Knowledge requirements
- Knowledge of the product’s configuration model, constraints, extension points, and known limits.
- Ability to identify fit-gap issues before they become late-stage surprises.
- Understanding of customer process variation and what can or cannot be supported without custom delivery.
- Ability to explain solution trade-offs in operational rather than purely technical language.
- Knowledge of data, access, and environment prerequisites for onboarding success.
- Ability to document configuration decisions so support and customer teams can reuse them.

## Experience requirements
- Has led customer workshops or configuration design for software onboarding.
- Has identified unsupported customer assumptions early enough to change the plan.
- Has produced fit-gap or configuration artifacts used in real implementation work.
- Has collaborated with product and engineering on customer-specific constraints.
- Has supported early-life use after onboarding to confirm the design held up.

## Decision rights
- Approve configuration baseline for the onboarding scope.
- Escalate fit-gap issues that require product or custom-delivery decisions.
- Reject customer requests that create unsupportable or unsafe configuration debt.
- Define configuration evidence needed for go-live readiness.

## Owned artifacts
- Configuration baseline
- Fit-gap log
- Environment prerequisite checklist

## Collaboration expectations
- Collaborate with onboarding manager, product owner, and customer stakeholders.
- Translate technical limits without hiding consequences.
- Ensure support and success teams understand configured behavior and caveats.
- Capture reusable patterns from repeated onboarding scenarios.

## Anti-patterns
- Promising configurability the product does not truly have.
- Keeping fit-gap issues informal until the cutover is near.
- Producing configuration notes that only the consultant can understand.
- Treating workarounds as equivalent to supported design.

## Fitness evidence
- Configuration artifacts reused successfully by delivery or support teams.
- Fit-gap decisions documented with clear consequences.
- Evidence of avoided rework because unsupported assumptions were surfaced early.
- Customer or internal feedback that the consultant reduced ambiguity.
