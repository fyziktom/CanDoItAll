# Kickoff lead

**Key:** `kickoff-lead`  
**Scope:** local  
**Process:** customer-onboarding  
**Preferred executor:** person  
**Preferred project role:** Manager  
**Seniority:** Senior implementation lead or onboarding program lead  
**Minimum years in primary discipline:** 5  
**Minimum years in software delivery:** 7

## Summary
Meeting and execution lead for the formal onboarding kickoff and first-phase alignment.

## Purpose
Run the kickoff decision point so participants leave with explicit owners, timeline, risks, and next actions.

## Staffing intent
A facilitation-capable implementation lead who can challenge ambiguity before the customer-facing kickoff is accepted as complete.

## Snapshot summary
Meeting and execution lead for onboarding kickoff and first-phase alignment.

## Domain tags
kickoff, facilitation, customer-onboarding, execution-alignment

## Knowledge requirements
- Deep understanding of software delivery planning, dependency mapping, and capacity-aware scheduling.
- Ability to distinguish optimistic plans from executable plans using concrete staffing and evidence signals.
- Knowledge of release governance, escalation paths, and portfolio-level sequencing trade-offs.
- Understanding of workflow bottlenecks such as missing approvals, environment readiness, and cross-team handoffs.
- Ability to read risk signals from burndown, blocked work, quality evidence, and unresolved decision records.
- Knowledge of how to use governance cadence without turning it into performative paperwork.

## Experience requirements
- Has coordinated at least one multi-team delivery with conflicting priorities and hard release constraints.
- Has re-planned work in flight when assumptions changed, including staffing, dependency, or customer deadline shifts.
- Has facilitated executive or governance escalation with a clear options-and-consequences framing.
- Has managed delivery cadence where approval, QA, and security evidence all materially affected release timing.
- Has worked closely with engineering and product to convert strategic goals into short-horizon delivery decisions.

## Decision rights
- Approve or reject execution plans that do not have credible staffing or dependency coverage.
- Escalate schedule, staffing, or governance risk before downstream teams are forced into silent failure.
- Choose the operating cadence and checkpoint rhythm for the process instance.
- Require explicit fallback ownership for critical roles and high-risk steps.

## Owned artifacts
- Delivery plan
- Dependency ledger
- Escalation log
- Release readiness summary

## Collaboration expectations
- Coordinate daily or agreed checkpoint-level alignment with role owners.
- Keep product, engineering, QA, and release stakeholders synchronized on risk and decision timing.
- Drive issue resolution instead of only reporting issue existence.
- Protect specialists from unnecessary context churn by front-loading coordination.

## Anti-patterns
- Publishing optimistic dates without verifying staffing and dependency reality.
- Escalating too late because of a desire to appear in control.
- Treating governance checkpoints as calendar events rather than decision points.
- Using vague status language that hides whether a step is blocked, risky, or truly on track.

## Fitness evidence
- Examples of delivery plans with explicit dependency and fallback treatment.
- Evidence of re-planning under pressure without losing traceability.
- Retrospective notes showing the role holder converted process friction into improvements.
- Consistent stakeholder trust in status signals produced by the role.
