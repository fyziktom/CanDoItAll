# Product owner

**Key:** `product-owner`  
**Scope:** shared  
**Process:** shared  
**Preferred executor:** person  
**Preferred project role:** CustomerContact  
**Seniority:** Staff or principal product leadership  
**Minimum years in primary discipline:** 6  
**Minimum years in software delivery:** 8

## Summary
Outcome and acceptance owner for value, scope, sequencing, and release acceptance.

## Purpose
Convert business intent into an explicit delivery contract with clear acceptance boundaries and prioritized value trade-offs.

## Staffing intent
One accountable human decision-maker who can clarify scope, decide priority, accept delivered outcomes, and absorb stakeholder escalation without routing every decision through engineering.

## Snapshot summary
Outcome and acceptance owner for value, scope, sequencing, and release acceptance.

## Domain tags
product-strategy, backlog-governance, acceptance-management

## Knowledge requirements
- Ability to express measurable product outcomes, acceptance criteria, and non-goals without ambiguity.
- Working knowledge of product discovery, delivery sequencing, and release trade-off economics.
- Understanding of dependency risk, technical debt implications, and how late scope changes affect delivery confidence.
- Ability to read architecture and QA evidence well enough to make informed accept/reject decisions.
- Understanding of regulatory, privacy, and contractual constraints that materially shape release scope.
- Ability to make fast decisions when evidence is incomplete, while still documenting rationale and residual risk.

## Experience requirements
- Has led at least two end-to-end software initiatives from scoped demand through released capability and post-release review.
- Has operated as the final business acceptance authority on production changes with multiple delivery teams involved.
- Has resolved cross-functional scope disputes between engineering, QA, commercial, and customer-facing stakeholders.
- Has handled late-breaking change requests and consciously traded feature ambition against schedule or risk.
- Has participated in incident or defect triage after release and translated lessons into backlog or policy change.

## Decision rights
- Approve or reject requested scope changes before implementation starts.
- Authorize business-priority trade-offs when schedule, quality, or dependency constraints conflict.
- Accept or reject completed delivery based on evidence, not optimism.
- Escalate unresolved scope or contractual ambiguity before implementation proceeds.

## Owned artifacts
- Intake brief
- Acceptance criteria pack
- Value statement
- Release acceptance note

## Collaboration expectations
- Work with delivery manager and architect to ensure business commitments are technically realistic.
- Respond to clarification requests within the agreed lead time so execution is not stalled by avoidable ambiguity.
- Review QA and security evidence before approving production-facing decisions.
- Communicate rejected scope or changed priorities explicitly to downstream teams.

## Anti-patterns
- Delegating every substantive decision after being assigned as the outcome owner.
- Accepting delivery based on verbal assurance without checking evidence.
- Introducing major scope change after implementation has started without reopening planning and risk review.
- Treating priority as fixed when new evidence shows a different path protects customer value better.

## Fitness evidence
- Examples of written acceptance decisions with explicit rationale.
- Evidence of managing a production release or equivalent change acceptance board.
- Traceable backlog and prioritization artifacts owned by the role holder.
- Positive feedback from engineering and QA that the role provides timely, concrete decisions.
