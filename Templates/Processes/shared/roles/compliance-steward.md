# Compliance steward

**Key:** `compliance-steward`  
**Scope:** shared  
**Process:** shared  
**Preferred executor:** person  
**Preferred project role:** Reviewer  
**Seniority:** Senior compliance, legal-ops, or governance specialist  
**Minimum years in primary discipline:** 7  
**Minimum years in software delivery:** 9

## Summary
Policy and evidence steward for contractual, regulatory, license, and audit-sensitive change decisions.

## Purpose
Ensure required compliance considerations are identified early, translated into actionable checks, and retained as evidence.

## Staffing intent
A governance specialist who understands how policy obligations intersect with real delivery work.

## Snapshot summary
Policy and evidence steward for contractual, regulatory, license, and audit-sensitive change decisions.

## Domain tags
compliance, policy, audit, license-obligations

## Knowledge requirements
- Knowledge of the organization’s key contractual, regulatory, privacy, and license obligations relevant to software change.
- Ability to translate abstract policy language into concrete delivery checkpoints and evidence expectations.
- Understanding of retention, access, sensitivity, and auditability requirements for artifacts.
- Ability to separate mandatory controls from good-practice guidance and communicate the difference clearly.
- Knowledge of escalation paths for exceptions, waivers, or unresolved compliance gaps.
- Ability to work constructively with engineering and product without turning governance into gridlock.

## Experience requirements
- Has supported or owned an audit, certification, or compliance evidence exercise tied to software delivery.
- Has reviewed change or release material for compliance-sensitive decisions.
- Has handled at least one exception, waiver, or policy interpretation dispute in a delivery context.
- Has worked with legal, security, or product stakeholders on evidence retention or usage restrictions.
- Has improved checklist or evidence design after audit or release pain.

## Decision rights
- Approve or reject compliance completeness for covered change types.
- Require retention, sensitivity labeling, or usage restrictions on artifacts.
- Escalate unresolved policy conflicts before release or customer commitment.
- Define expiry or review expectations for approved exceptions.

## Owned artifacts
- Compliance review note
- Exception request
- Retention requirement note
- Audit evidence map

## Collaboration expectations
- Work with security, OSS, and release roles where obligations overlap.
- Clarify policy early enough that teams can still adapt their implementation plan.
- Document exact compliance expectations rather than vague 'needs review' statements.
- Support retrospective improvement when policy handling created avoidable friction.

## Anti-patterns
- Introducing compliance requirements after work is complete without prior warning.
- Applying blanket rules without checking whether they truly apply.
- Storing sensitive review conclusions in artifacts with the wrong access scope.
- Approving exceptions without expiry, ownership, or rationale.

## Fitness evidence
- Audit-ready evidence maps or compliance summaries owned by the role holder.
- Examples of policy interpretations accepted by delivery teams and auditors.
- Reduction in avoidable compliance rework after steward involvement.
- Clear retention and usage restrictions on sensitive governance artifacts.
