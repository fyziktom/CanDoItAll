# Incident commander

**Key:** `incident-commander`  
**Scope:** shared  
**Process:** shared  
**Preferred executor:** person  
**Preferred project role:** Manager  
**Seniority:** Senior incident or operations leadership  
**Minimum years in primary discipline:** 8  
**Minimum years in software delivery:** 10

## Summary
Crisis-flow owner for coordination, decision timing, and containment during active incidents.

## Purpose
Drive containment and structured decision-making when time pressure and uncertainty are high.

## Staffing intent
A senior incident leader who can coordinate specialists, protect signal quality, and make time-sensitive trade-offs.

## Snapshot summary
Crisis-flow owner for coordination, decision timing, and containment during active incidents.

## Domain tags
incident-management, coordination, containment

## Knowledge requirements
- Ability to run time-critical coordination while maintaining factual discipline.
- Knowledge of incident severity frameworks, escalation protocols, and communication cadence.
- Understanding of containment, rollback, degraded-mode, and forensic preservation trade-offs.
- Ability to identify when a technical thread requires a business or communications decision.
- Knowledge of stakeholder management under pressure, including executives and customers.
- Ability to prevent chaos by assigning explicit owners and decision deadlines.

## Experience requirements
- Has led or co-led a real production incident or equivalent crisis response.
- Has coordinated technical, support, and communications workstreams simultaneously.
- Has made containment decisions under uncertainty and documented the rationale.
- Has facilitated post-incident review and follow-through on corrective actions.
- Has dealt with high-pressure stakeholder escalation without losing factual discipline.

## Decision rights
- Set incident cadence, priorities, and escalation thresholds.
- Approve containment direction and stop uncoordinated interventions.
- Escalate customer, legal, or executive impact when the incident crosses agreed thresholds.
- Own the decision to move from active response to learning and recovery mode.

## Owned artifacts
- Incident timeline
- Containment decision log
- Executive status note
- Post-incident improvement charter

## Collaboration expectations
- Coordinate specialists without micromanaging their technical work.
- Keep product/service owners aware of impact and likely decision windows.
- Work with communications roles so outward messaging tracks technical reality.
- Protect scribe and forensic roles so evidence is not lost under pressure.

## Anti-patterns
- Acting as the loudest technical contributor instead of the coordinator.
- Allowing parallel unapproved fixes that worsen the incident.
- Optimizing for speed at the cost of evidence preservation when that evidence is needed.
- Declaring the incident done before ownership of follow-through is clear.

## Fitness evidence
- Incident command logs showing explicit ownership and decision timing.
- Retrospectives that attribute improved containment or coordination to the role holder.
- Evidence of calm, factual stakeholder communication under pressure.
- Corrective actions actually delivered after incidents the role owned.
