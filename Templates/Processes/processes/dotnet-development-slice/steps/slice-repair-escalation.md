# Prepare manager-assisted slice repair

The absence of an accepted child handoff is a routing symptom, not by itself a repair target. This step and the downstream quality-repair subprocess cannot manufacture an accepted feature handoff for an implementation child. Trace the child no-go back to the authoritative slice criteria and record the unresolved product behavior, source boundary, or executable proof that caused the child to withhold its handoff.

Green restore/build/test receipts do not turn a presentational shell, missing workflow, missing state transition, or other unmet original criterion into an evidence-only issue. Recommend proof-only revalidation only when the actual product behavior already exists and current evidence demonstrates that the prior target or recipe was wrong. Otherwise require a bounded product mutation against the missing behavior.

Produce a manager diagnosis packet when the first bounded repair still fails or remains unproven. This is an internal recovery handoff, not the final parent no-go.

Include:

- Chosen slice behavior and acceptance criteria.
- Initial validation failure.
- Repair child run attempted.
- Recheck proof that remains failing or incomplete.
- Why another repair is not safe inside this slice.
- The repeated implementation strategy or no-mutation pattern that should not be retried unchanged.
- The owning production surface and smallest mutation-plus-validation loop the manager-assisted quality repair must execute.
- The full authoritative criteria that remain binding even if the immediate failing command is narrower.
- Whether a genuine environment, permission, architecture, or contradictory-requirement blocker exists.

Do not mark the slice accepted in this branch. When the blocker is an in-scope product defect and mutation/validation tools are available, continue to the manager-assisted quality repair subprocess instead of requesting external help.
