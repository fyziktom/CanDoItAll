# Training readiness owner

**Key:** `training-readiness-owner`  
**Scope:** local  
**Process:** software-delivery  
**Preferred executor:** person  
**Preferred project role:** Stakeholder  
**Seniority:** Senior enablement or operations readiness specialist  
**Minimum years in primary discipline:** 4  
**Minimum years in software delivery:** 6

## Summary
Owner for end-user enablement, release notes clarity, and operational adoption readiness.

## Purpose
Ensure release output is accompanied by the minimal training, release notes, and internal enablement needed for successful adoption.

## Staffing intent
A delivery or enablement specialist who can turn technical change into usable guidance for affected users and support teams.

## Snapshot summary
Owner for end-user enablement, release notes clarity, and operational adoption readiness.

## Domain tags
enablement, training, release-notes, adoption

## Knowledge requirements
- Ability to identify what users, support, and customer-facing teams need to understand before or immediately after release.
- Knowledge of release notes design, training format selection, and stakeholder communication timing.
- Understanding of adoption friction caused by missing documentation or ambiguous behavior changes.
- Ability to extract accurate behavior deltas from engineering and product input.
- Knowledge of support-readiness and knowledge-base update workflows.
- Ability to define minimum enablement completeness criteria.

## Experience requirements
- Has owned training or enablement deliverables for a software launch or major feature.
- Has coordinated release-note or support-content updates with technical teams.
- Has handled rollout where adoption risk was driven by user misunderstanding rather than technical defect.
- Has updated internal support teams on changed workflows or known caveats.
- Has measured or at least qualitatively assessed readiness gaps after release.

## Decision rights
- Approve completion of required enablement artifacts before broad rollout.
- Require documentation updates for behavior changes that affect users or support.
- Escalate if release timing would make support or adoption chaos likely.
- Declare whether a limited rollout can proceed without full broad-audience training.

## Owned artifacts
- Release notes
- Training deck
- Support readiness note

## Collaboration expectations
- Collaborate with product owner, customer success, and QA on customer-facing clarity.
- Work with engineers to make technical change understandable without distortion.
- Coordinate with service owner for support window planning.
- Feed adoption pain back into retrospective improvements.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating release notes as marketing copy instead of operational guidance.
- Publishing training content that does not match the implemented behavior.
- Declaring readiness because documentation exists, regardless of usefulness.
- Ignoring support-team readiness for changed workflows.

## Fitness evidence
- Adoption artifacts consumed successfully by customer-facing teams.
- Release note and training materials with explicit behavioral changes.
- Evidence of reduced support confusion after guided releases.
- Retrospective feedback showing the role improved adoption readiness.
