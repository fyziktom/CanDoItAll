# Incident scribe

**Key:** `incident-scribe`  
**Scope:** local  
**Process:** incident-response  
**Preferred executor:** person  
**Preferred project role:** Stakeholder  
**Seniority:** Experienced support, operations, or PMO contributor  
**Minimum years in primary discipline:** 3  
**Minimum years in software delivery:** 5

## Summary
Structured recorder for live decisions, assignments, and status changes during incident response.

## Purpose
Preserve a reliable operational memory of what was decided, by whom, and when, so responders can focus without losing traceability.

## Staffing intent
A disciplined operator dedicated to live note-taking, timeline hygiene, and decision capture.

## Snapshot summary
Structured recorder for live decisions, assignments, and status changes during incident response.

## Domain tags
incident-logging, note-taking, decision-traceability

## Knowledge requirements
- Ability to capture decisions, owners, timestamps, and open actions accurately in fast-moving situations.
- Knowledge of incident cadence, meeting hygiene, and what details are worth preserving.
- Understanding of the difference between claimed status and confirmed status.
- Ability to keep notes structured enough for later forensic and retrospective use.
- Knowledge of confidentiality and audience boundaries for incident notes.
- Ability to prompt for missing ownership or unclear decisions without disrupting flow.

## Experience requirements
- Has supported live incident bridges, war rooms, or rapid-response coordination.
- Has maintained accurate action logs or timelines under pressure.
- Has turned raw live notes into usable post-incident documentation.
- Has worked with executives or technical leaders in time-critical meetings.
- Has enforced discipline around owner/time/next-step capture.

## Decision rights
- Require explicit owner and due time when decisions are made.
- Escalate when the response lacks a clear action log.
- Control the official incident note set and versioning.
- Flag contradictions between successive status statements.

## Owned artifacts
- Live action log
- Incident notes
- Decision timeline appendix

## Collaboration expectations
- Work beside the incident commander without becoming a bottleneck.
- Coordinate with forensics analyst on timeline integrity.
- Provide communications roles with accurate, approved status inputs.
- Hand off clean notes for the post-incident review.

## Anti-patterns
- Trying to solve the incident instead of preserving decision quality.
- Capturing minutes without owners or timestamps.
- Editing history to make the response look cleaner after the fact.
- Publishing notes to the wrong audience.

## Fitness evidence
- High-quality incident notes consumed after the event.
- Post-incident reviews that rely on the scribe record confidently.
- Evidence of improved action follow-through due to disciplined logging.
- Trust from incident leaders in the scribe’s accuracy.
